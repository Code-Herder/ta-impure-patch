# ImpAI in the engine

ImpAI's seam, its seat table, the seat choices -- `seatai` and the SKIRMISH seat button that writes it, a network game's AI names and the battle room's button that sets them -- what each of its seats knows and the self-checks that hold that to the engine, the per-type threat table, the type information and the build menus, the seat's own site test, the debug overlay and the cost instrument, the Difficulty handicap's exemption and the console's `AI` command (`tagpu_impai.c`, `tagpu_impai.h`, `tagpu_skirmish.c`, `impai_seats.c`; the engine-free half `impai.h`, `impai_know.c`, `impai_maps.c`, `impai_tinfo.c` and the brain `impai_brain.c`, `impai_econ.c`, `impai_build.c`; the engine's reads `tagpu_worldq.c`). ImpAI plays the seats it is given -- what each knows, and the brain's economy: its builders, its factories, its expansion to metal -- and takes them out of the handicap; the stock AI plays every other computer seat (*The seam and the wrapper*, *The brain*). The plan, the engine facts it rests on and what ImpAI will do are [entering the game](../impai/engine-seam.html) and [ImpAI for the player](../impai/player.html).

*A module note: it states the module as it is now. A landing that changes the module edits its section here in place; what the landing measured and what its review found go to the commit, its `landing-review:` note and the gate's plan note.*

Addresses are VAs for our pristine build (ImageBase `0x400000`). `main` = `*(void**)0x511DE8`; the player records are at `main+0x1B63`, stride `0x14B`, ten of them; `+0x73` is the controller (1 human, 2 AI, 3 remote or a replay puppet).

## The seat choice: `seatai`

`impure.cfg`'s key `seatai` is `none`, or the seats ImpAI plays as digits 0–9 separated by commas (`seatai=1,3`): bit i for settings entry i, which is record i on every path that seats a skirmish ([sim](../engine/sim.html), *Seating a level*). Anything else logs `settings: seatai: <text> not understood - no seat plays ImpAI` and reads as none. `tagpu_settings_seatai` answers it under `tagpu_defaults.off` too, as the engine honours its own SKIRMISH registry keys under every launch. The DLL writes it from one place, the seat button below (`tagpu_settings_set_seatai`, under every launch too, so a click under `tagpu_defaults.off` rewrites `impure.cfg` from the values read at attach); tacli and the player write it too. **Every tacli launch writes it** (`write_harness_store`, and the remote launch): the seats a scenario names `impai` (`tacli scenario load`) or `tacli launch --seatai 1,3` names, or `none`, so no launch plays an earlier launch's seats ([the scenario format](../scenario-format.html)).

**The seat button** (`tagpu_skirmish.c`): on the SKIRMISH screen a computer seat takes two steps, `OTA AI` then `ImpAI`, so `Player<N>` cycles **Open → OTA AI → ImpAI → Player** (when no seat is the player, the engine's own rule) **→ Open**. The engine's controller value stays 2 for both; the click sets or clears the seat's `seatai` bit and writes the label. Two call-site redirects, byte-matched at attach, both or neither, and neither when ImpAI is unavailable (the screen is then stock): `0x47B178`, the handler's call of the cycle `0x4797E0` — a click on a seat at its first computer step moves it to the second without the cycle, every other click runs the cycle, a seat entering Computer takes the first step and one leaving it loses its bit — and `0x47A1FC`, the screen init's label call for a Computer seat, which writes `ImpAI` or `OTA AI` by the bit. The seat is bounded below 10 and the screen's seat count `main+0x38D81`, else the click is stock's. `IMPAI_FIRST_COMPUTER` (0: OTA AI first) is the one constant that orders the two steps. Attach logs `skirmish: the seat button steps a computer seat through OTA AI and ImpAI (0x47B178, 0x47A1FC)` or why it stays stock ([ui](../engine/ui.html), *The SKIRMISH screen's handler*).

**A saved game does not carry the choice.** The save holds the records' controllers, not the seat table, so a reloaded game seats ImpAI from the `seatai` in force when it loads, and the seats its level-init line names can differ from the game that was saved. The save's players restore `0x466050` also writes `+0x73` (`0x4662C3`) after the level init, so the line can name a seat that is not controller 2 in play; every reader re-tests controller 2, so such a mark does nothing.

## The network choice: the seat's name

In a network game the choice is **the AI seat's own name**: `ImpAI:<human>` for a seat ImpAI plays, the engine's `AI:<human>` for OTA AI, both cut to 16 characters as the engine's add cuts every AI name (`0x45131E`). The name is part of the player record, so it moves with the seat when the battle room compacts its rows by moving records ([network](../engine/network.html), *The room compacts its rows by moving records*), a table kept by row would not; and DirectPlay carries it to every other peer, so every peer's room and log say which AI a seat is. `impai_seats.c` holds the rule and the names, three-build tested.

**The button** (`tagpu_skirmish.c`, *THE BATTLE ROOM*): a click on the peer's own AI row — which stock answers, past its one-second guard, by removing the AI — first steps the row to the other AI and renames it, and the next click removes it as stock does. The cycle is **Unused → ImpAI → OTA AI → Unused** with `netai=impai`, **Unused → OTA AI → ImpAI → Unused** with `netai=ota`, the host's with Blocked before them. Stock's one AI a peer (`0x457B90`) is kept: the step changes which AI plays a row, not how many rows there are. Neither of its two writes is made while ImpAI is not available: a `jmp` at `0x447CB3` on the removal branch, after the handler's own tests (active, type 2, older than 30 ticks), and — when ImpAI is the first step — the immediate of the add's `push 0x5064B4` at `0x451310`, so the add names the row `ImpAI:%s`, never written without the `jmp` and put back when the `jmp` cannot be landed. The step renames through the engine's own rename `0x450090(row, short, long)`, which has no caller in retail: it writes both names bounded to 30 bytes and sends DirectPlay's SetPlayerName, whose system message `0x103` each other peer copies into its own record of the seat. The label follows with no write of ours: the click's tail sets the room's dirty bit, and the row refresh `0x448C70` sets every `PLAYER<n>` text from its record's `+0x2B`. Attach logs `skirmish: the battle room's AI row steps ImpAI, then OTA AI, then Unused (0x447CB3, 0x451310)` or why the room stays stock.

**`netai`** (`impure.cfg`, `ota` by default, or `impai`) is the first computer step, read once at attach and answered under `tagpu_defaults.off` too, as `seatai` is. Nothing in the DLL writes it. Every tacli launch writes it — `ota`, or `impai` with `tacli launch --netai impai` — so no launch inherits an earlier one's. Each peer reads only its own store.

**The checks**, each one two-peer game on Two Continents (`tools/impai/twopeers.py`: the run's two instances on a private display, a leased DirectPlay port, two game lanes of the run lock). `tools/impai/netai_check.py` launches the host with `--netai impai` and the joiner with `--netai ota`, each adds one computer seat, and judges from both logs and both peers' records that each peer marks only the seat it added and named `ImpAI:`, that the other peer names it among its other peers' ImpAI seats, that both log level 2, and that only the host logs ImpAI turns (with `--pulse`, pulse lines). `tools/impai/netroom_check.py` launches both with `--netai impai`, clicks each peer's AI row through its cycle and reads every `PLAYER<n>` label on both peers after each click, then starts and checks the marks. `tools/impai/netprobe.py` measures the engine facts the network branch rests on (the network Difficulty, the activation, a departed peer's seats; [network](../engine/network.html)) and judges nothing of Impure. Their engine-free halves are in `tools/impai/test_impai_tools.py`.

## The install

At attach, after `tagpu_reclaim_init` and `tagpu_replay_init`, whatever the levers, and only when the level's two edges are ours:

- replay's play sites are in (`tagpu_replay_level_init_in`), among them the level init's `PLAYERS` call `0x4919C8`, retargeted to `tagpu_replay_level_players`, the only caller of ImpAI's level init;
- the teardown wrap `0x491B60` is in (`tagpu_reclaim_level_tracked`), the only caller of ImpAI's level end.

Then it compares twenty sites with their stock bytes: the seam `0x465031`, the `AI` command's two calls `0x4162F4` and `0x4162FE`, the sixteen handicap sites and the site test's `0x47D3D1`. Only when every one matches does it build the stubs and the seat's site-test thunk, in one `SIM_CODE` page, and write all twenty, putting back every site it wrote if a write fails; only then does `WQ_SITE_SEAT` get the thunk. Success logs `impai: seam 0x465031, the AI command 0x4162F4 and 0x4162FE, 16 handicap sites and the seat's site test 0x47D3D1 in; seats at 0x<addr>, handicap counts at 0x<addr>` (the addresses `handicap_check.py` peeks). Otherwise nothing is written, ImpAI is unavailable, and the attach and every level log `impai: unavailable: <why>`, a mismatch as `<VA> not stock (<bytes>)`. The sites' rows are in [the hook map](hook-map.html), *The AI seat*.

**The test levers** are read at attach by `tagpu_impai_levers`, before the simulation registry closes its lever list, so a recording lists each: `tagpu_impai_check.on` turns on the self-checks (*The self-checks*, below), `tagpu_impaiov.on` the overlay (*The overlay*), `tagpu_impai_budget.on` the cost run's raised budgets (*The cost*). With it, once every site above is in, the install also retargets the radar pass's call `0x46556D` (`E8 CE 1E 00 00`, byte-matched) and logs `impai: check: line of sight in every ImpAI turn, radar at 0x46556D`, or why the radar check is out. Without it `0x46556D` stays stock.

## The seam and the wrapper

The players phase's `call 0x408C40` at `0x465031` calls `impai_ai_tick`, `__fastcall(obj, edx)`: ECX arrives as its first argument and it pops nothing, so the site's stack is unchanged, and `EBX`, `ESI`, `EDI` and `EBP`, which the players phase uses after the call, are callee-saved. The AI object's seat is ImpAI's (`impai_seat_marked`) when all of these hold:

- **the bound**: `rec = *(obj+0)` lies at `main+0x1B63 + i·0x14B`, `i` below 10, by range and exact division, before anything is read through it;
- the record is seated with controller 2, the test `0x408C40` makes before it plans;
- the seat table marks record `i`, and `i` is not a puppet seat.

Any other seat takes the stock tick `0x408C40`, and the recorder's observer on its entry sees the call. **An ImpAI seat never runs `0x408C40`'s group assignment `0x408830` or its ten planners**, on either thread: of the tick it runs only the weapon scan `0x4089A0`, called by address so that the extra-weapons module's detour of its entry runs, and with argument 0, as `0x408C40` ends for every record but an active type-2 one: CommandFire weapons are left to orders (`0x408A88..0x408A96`; the detour's port tests the same argument), where the stock AI's argument 1 (`0x408C96..0x408C9A`) fires them on its own. On the **loader thread's** one players phase (`0x497C10`) that is all it runs. On the **game thread** (`GetCurrentThreadId()` against `g_ddraw.gui_thread_id`) the seat's turn -- its observation (*What a seat knows*), then the brain on its decision tick (*The brain*) -- and the scan run inside `tagpu_rec_ai_turn_enter`/`_leave`, the recorder's attribution bracket beside `ai_enter`: the orders the turn builds are the seat's in the recorder's AI activity window, and the scan's detour ends the attribution before it scans (`tagpu_rec_ai_planning_end`), as for the stock tick.

## What a seat knows

Each ImpAI seat keeps, for every unit slot, what it may know of the unit there: `s_known[slot].seat[i]`, 8 bytes (`impai.h`, `IMPAI_SEEN`): the type and owner last identified (`0xFFFF` and `0xFF` when never), the classes it holds the slot by now (`how`: own, line of sight, shared radar, radar, sonar), and the 32-px block last known. An all-zero row is empty. **The information rule**: a row changes only through what the seat observes, so a death or a slot reuse the seat cannot see changes nothing it knows, and no engine fact reaches a decision through a row. A row with no class left is a **ghost**: the last thing known, kept at its block until the seat sees that block again, when it is emptied.

**One turn** (`impai_observe`, then the engine-free `impai_know_tick` over the engine's WORLDQ, `tagpu_worldq.c`), at the seat's place in the players phase, after the step's unit tick, projectiles and explosions and before the seat's own sight emits, so the view is the start of the seat's turn at this tick; nothing after the players phase in the step writes a position, the alive bit, an owner, a type or cloak (DISASSEMBLED for the direct calls, [INFERRED for indirect calls]: [sim](../engine/sim.html), the step):

1. **The line-of-sight slice**: `OBS_SLICE` (2048) slots from the seat's cursor, wrapping. `observe` answers the first class that holds: **own** (the owner byte is the seat); **line of sight**, `0x465AC0`'s rule for the seat — not cloaked, not submerged, then the four points of the unit's box ([map](../engine/map.html), `0x465AC0`) at the live `fix_los_shear` row (one of the fail-closed table's, which is installed whole at attach or the process ends at the first DirectDraw call; the copy gives no sight unless `tagpu_limits_in` says the table is in, so it reads the fixed rule only where the engine runs it), read in the seat's counters under True line of sight and in the mapped grid **with the seat's own bit** `rec+0x146` otherwise (the engine's own reads take the viewed player's bit whatever record asks; the stamp writes every record's); **shared**, loop 1 of the radar pass for the seat (the owner's record allies the seat and shares radar) — never in a skirmish, where ShareRadar cannot be set. Own and line of sight identify the type and owner; shared gives the block alone. **A submerged unit is never identified**: the engine sees one only by `+0x110` bit 9, the viewed player's sonar; the seat knows it through its own sonar.
2. **The radar pass**: started when idle and `GameTime % 30 == (3·seat + 1) % 30`, so two seats never start on one tick, and continued every tick until it completes, at most `RADAR_VISITS` (32768) target visits a tick, rounded up to the end of the walk in progress: an emitter's, or one of a jammer's two (its radar radius, then its sonar radius), each started only while the budget lasts, so a tick ends at most one walk past it. It is `0x467440`'s five loops for the seat ([recorder](../engine/recorder.html), `0x467440`; loop 4, the same for every viewer, left out), walking the engine's own bucket chains as `0x47E890` does, since a unit's bucket can lag its position and a carried unit is in none. Its answer, a nibble a slot in `s_radar_bits[seat]` (radar, sonar, jammed, kept), is published into the rows when it completes: a contact takes the radar and sonar classes, and the block when no class of now holds it; a slot the pass did not find loses them. **A contact is the unit the pass found**: every bit the pass sets writes the slot's record in `s_radar_hit` (`WQ_RADAR_HIT`, 16 bytes; every seat's record of the slot in one 160-byte row, `IMPAI_HITROW`, and each seat's column passed to the pass with its stride, `WQ_HITS`) -- the unit's incarnation, `tagpu_unit_birth`, the stale-hits fix's per-slot stamp when it is an exact birth, which rises strictly from one unit of a slot to the next (an unknown or lower-bound stamp is none), and its block then -- and the publish takes a bit only while the slot still holds that incarnation, at the recorded block. So a unit that died while the pass was open, its slot gone to a unit the seat cannot see, makes no contact, and one that moved on is where the pass saw it; no live position reaches a row through a contact. A slot that changes hands while the pass is open loses the bits the pass set on its earlier unit when the pass sets one on the later (`radar_kept`), so a contact takes only the classes its own unit was found by. The seat's own units get their incarnation alone, written only when it changes (`radar_mine`), so a pass rewrites no record of a unit the seat keeps. Without the fail-closed table there is no incarnation, and the seat publishes no radar contact at all. Under load a pass grows staler; a tick's work does not grow.
3. **The patch scan**: `CLU_ROWS` (16) map rows a tick through the game's own patch rule, `tasim_snap_patches` — the explored metal patches Quick Build tells apart, each flood at most `SNAP_PATCH_CAP` cells and the tick's at most `CLU_FLOOD` (`2 · SNAP_PATCH_CAP`) — into the second half of the seat's `s_cluster[seat]` (2 × 256 `SNAP_PATCH`), which replaces the first, published half at the last row, so a half-done scan is never read. A row whose floods need more than one tick's budget is passed over with its patches unreported, so the scan always advances. The flood's arena is `tagpu_sim_scratch`, freed before the turn returns.

**The content** -- the reach labels, the threat table, the type information and the build menus below -- is `SIM_CONTENT`, a function of what the load made, so it is built at the level init and nowhere else, on the loader thread, before any unit or feature of play changes the pathing maps it reads ([map](../engine/map.html), the pathing maps): on a live level when a seat is marked (*The seat table*: ImpAI available, no remote record, a seated controller-2 record whose `seatai` bit is set), and on a puppet level whenever ImpAI is available, so a takeover or a rewind of it plays on the content the live game had and builds none in play. Every other level has none, its tables emptied whole (`impai_content_clear`), so a game with no ImpAI seat pays nothing for them. A live level whose menus are past their tables has no content and marks no seat, so its header names none either.

**The reach labels** (`impai_reach_build`, `SIM_CONTENT`): at the level init (*The content*), live or puppet, on the loader thread inside PLAYERS, after `0x440940` has built the movement classes' pathing maps ([map](../engine/map.html), the pathing maps), each class the level has is labelled by 4-connectivity over its passable cells (code not 0) and each 32-px block takes the label of its first passable cell, 0 when none: one `u16` a block, class after class, in one block sized for the level's classes (`s_reach`, `s_reach_ix`) and freed at the next level init. A class whose map is not the PLOT grid's size is read as blocked everywhere. Units are not obstacles (none exists at PLAYERS); a saved game's features, restored after this point, are missing. The level init logs `impai: reach classes=<n> blocks=<w>x<h> components=<n> cells=<n> blocks_written=<n> us=<n>`: the work in counts (each class labels every cell once and reads its four neighbours, then writes every block) and, as a measurement, its time. MEASURED 2026-11-12 on Comet Catcher (384 × 480 cells, 15 classes, the harness's worker): 2 764 800 cells labelled and 691 200 blocks written in 21 ms. The work is linear in cells × classes, so a 2048 × 2048 map with 15 classes is about 0.5 s and 30 MB of labels, with 32 classes about 1 s and 64 MB [INFERRED by scaling, not run]; no bound beyond the classes' W and H holds it. A class with `IMPAI_REACH_MANY` components or more is dropped from the present classes and logged (`impai: reach class <c> dropped`), because past that count a label no longer names one component, so every class a reach test reads has equal labels only where its blocks connect. Beside the labels, **the block tops** (`s_reach_top`, `SIM_CONTENT`, one byte a block): the most of the feature grid's cell byte `+0x05` -- each cell's 2×2 height maximum, the byte the site test compares a water building's depth against ([orders](../engine/orders.html), the `0x47D2E0` row; [map](../engine/map.html), the `+0x04`..`+0x07` row) -- over the block's four cells, 255 for the last column and row, which `0x483210` never writes, and with no grid (`cell_top`); without their memory the level has none (`impai: reach tops none`) and no seat places a sea plant. After the type information, **the reach summary** (`impai_reach_summary`) counts the blocks some sea army class's labels cover, the land army types' weight by class and the sea class the sea plant searches by, and logs `impai: reach summary sea_blocks=<n> of <blocks> land_total=<n> sea_ref=<class|-1> mostly_water=<0|1>`. The labels answer over the whole map: the skirmish screen shows every seat the map's picture ([the brain](../impai/brain.html), *The naval rule*).

**The threat table** (`s_threat_def`, `SIM_CONTENT`, one 16-byte `IMPAI_THREAT` a unit type, `TAGPU_LIM_TYPES` rows, 256 KB): at the level init (*The content*), live or puppet, on the loader thread inside `0x4919C8`, `tagpu_worldq_role_in` reads every type's classifier input where the unit-data load `0x42D2E0` (`0x4918CA`) left it, and `tasim_roles_classify` classifies it by the rule `tasim_run roles` applies to the install's files ([the calibration runner](tasim-calibration.html), *Roles*); `impai_threat_from_roles` rounds damage per second against ground, air and water, the ground range, the value, the layer group and the role flags into the row. The input is the file filler's keys from where the engine keeps them ([keys](../engine/keys.html)): the def array below UNITINFOCount (flags `+0x241`/`+0x245` masked to the bits the classifier tests, `bmcode`, the costs, `maxdamage`, `workertime`, `maxvelocity`, the sensor words, the water depths `+0x1BE`/`+0x1C0`, `energyuse`, `makesmetal`, `transportcapacity`); `weapon1`..`weapon10` through the extra-weapons module, a slot whose record is the no-weapon entry left empty, each weapon's range `+0xDC`, reload `+0xE4`, burst `+0xEA`, default damage `+0xD4`, per-shot costs and flags `+0x111`, and Impure's `nottoair` from the weapon keys; the air damage, each weapon's mean over the types of category `VTOL` of its `[DAMAGE]` row for the type, else its default ([weapons](../engine/weapons.html), the table's layout); and each slot 0..2's bad-target tag, the name of the category-map entry whose mask the def holds at `+0x231 + 4k`, with NOTAIR among its words. The category map is walked, never looked up through `0x488C50`, which inserts on a miss ([units](../engine/units.html), *The unit-category map*). **Build options**, which the classifier's tech walk follows from the commanders: a builder's (`+0x241` bit 6) are its build menus (*The build menus*, built first for this) merged over the sides into one ascending list (`impai_menu_union`), without the builder itself, as the file filler leaves a builder's own type out; an option past the type count fails the fill. A builder's own pages are the same for every side and only a page it has none of comes from the seat's side's `DL` page, so the merge gives a builder every type a seat of any side can have it build; the file filler takes that page from the builder's own `side` key, which the engine keeps nowhere (read only by the pre-scan `0x42A8D0`, [keys](../engine/keys.html)), so a builder with no page of its own can differ between the two. The classifier's −1 (no commander, so no tech level) leaves every figure the row takes defined; a level whose input cannot be had has no table, and the maps draw no threat or influence. The level init logs `impai: threat types=<n>`; under the check lever also every type's role, figures and tech level, which `tools/impai/roles_check.py` compares with `tasim_run roles` type by type: equal on the retail install (278 types, MEASURED 2026-11-12 on Comet Catcher, before the tech level was compared) but for type 0, the engine's reserved type, which the log names `None` and no FBI defines. **The tech level's agreement is not yet measured in a game.** Both fillers run `tagpu_buildmenu.c` over the same pages and download entries, and they choose the pages differently -- the engine by its page count `+0x22E` and orders bit ([ui](../engine/ui.html), *The build pages*), the file filler by the pages that exist or an entry names. On the retail install the two choose the same pages for all 53 builders: none has an orders page `<NAME>0.GUI`, none a gap or a 0-byte page, and the 8 with no page of their own (ARMASP, ARMCARRY, ARMDECOM, ARMFARK and CORE's four) have no download entry either, so no `DL` page and no side is read (MEASURED 2026-11-13 over the install's archives through `tools/wiki/tavfs.py`, by the page-count rule above) [INFERRED from it: the same tech levels, 0x488B10's name match and the file filler's being the same names].

**The type information** (`s_tinfo`, `SIM_CONTENT`, one 24-byte `IMPAI_TINFO` a unit type): built beside the threat table from the same classification, by the engine-free `impai_tinfo_build` over the **content view** (`tagpu_worldq_open_content`: the defs and the map, no live unit, every site refused, so it opens on the loader thread, live or puppet). A type's category is the first that holds: commander (the classifier's flag), extractor or geothermal (its kind), factory (the classifier's flag), constructor (a mobile builder with a worker time), maker (a building with `makesmetal` and `energyuse` above 0), energy (an unarmed building whose energy gain is above 0), other; its gains are a 30-tick pass's on this map (the wind's mean and the tide from the map: the loader body writes the wind's divisor at `0x4918ED` and calls the wind step at `0x491903`, both before `PLAYERS`); its value is metal plus energy over the classifier's ratio; its tech level is the classifier's, the least number of factories before it on a build chain from a commander and at least 1, 0 when no chain reaches it ([the calibration runner](tasim-calibration.html), *Roles*). So a plant is one tech above the factories before it: on retail every plant a tech-1 constructor builds is tech 1 like the lab that made the constructor (ARMLAB and ARMALAB both 1), and only a plant a tech-2 constructor builds is tech 2 (ARMPLAT, CORGANT; `tasim_run roles`, MEASURED 2026-11-13). The level init logs `impai: tinfo mex=<n> energy=<n> maker=<n> factory=<n> cons=<n>`, or `impai: tinfo none: …` when the view cannot be opened.

**The build menus** (`s_menu`, `s_menu_pool`, `SIM_CONTENT`, their live rows, each row's builder and every pool entry a type ID by their layouts): for every side the game loaded (`main+0x37F39`, at most 5), every builder type's (def `+0x241` bit 6) build menu, built first of the level's content but the reach labels, since the threat table's classification reads them, read by Quick Build's page rule (`tagpu_resource.c`, `tagpu_buildmenu_types`, on the loader thread after the defs and the download records are loaded) with every type a press reaches: the sidebar arms a type whose def `+0x22F` (`bmcode`) is 0 (`0x41AB7F`) and hands any other to the factory's queue (`0x41AC3C`, `0x419B00`; [ui](../engine/ui.html), the build press), and a factory's menu holds only the second kind, so Quick Build's own rule, the armed types alone, leaves every factory's menu empty and no plant can be chosen, sorted by (side, builder) into 2048 rows and a 65 536-type pool. Every side, not the marked seats' alone, so any mark the level can come to hold -- its own, a takeover's, a rewind's -- finds its side's menus: in the compatibility suite's ImpAI stages a side's factories, constructors and commanders took at most 148 builders and 1309 types (MEASURED 2026-11-13), so five take under half of either table [INFERRED for the sides no stage built, and for builders of none of those categories, which that measure left out]. Every builder, not those three categories alone: the categories are the classifier's answer, so they cannot choose the rows the classifier is fed. All or none: past either table the level init logs `impai: menus past their tables (…)`, the level has no content and marks no seat. Each side logs `impai: menus side=<s> builders=<n> types=<n> ms=<t>`. Two readers, one table (`impai_menu_find`): `tagpu_impai_menu` is ImpAI's `WqMenuFn`, the seat's side, a binary search, a copy; `impai_menu_union` is the threat build's, a builder's rows of every side merged.

**The maps** (`impai_maps.c`, `impai_maps_build`), engine-free and derived on demand, never stored: over one seat's rows, its patches, the threat table and the map size, at 256-px cells (at most 128 × 128) — **threat** per layer (ground, air, water), each identified enemy row's damage per second stamped on the cells whose centres lie within its type's range of its block's centre; **influence**, own value minus identified enemies', summed over each cell's 3 × 3 box; **presence**, enemy rows known only by radar or as ghosts. Only the overlay builds them in the game yet.

The knowledge (`s_known`, keyed by unit slot, `TAGPU_PK_DESIGN_SLOTS` rows of 80 bytes), each seat's cursors and pass (`s_seatst`), the passes' bits and records (`s_radar_bits`, `s_radar_hit`) and the patches (`s_cluster`) are `SIM_STATE`, cleared at every level init; a puppet's come from its recording. `s_radar_hit` is a `SIM_BLOCK` of a fixed `TAGPU_PK_DESIGN_SLOTS` rows, allocated at attach in a block of its own (about 2.4 MB, never freed), so the recorder write-watches it and images only the pages a pass wrote, and every level records and restores the same rows. It has no count: a replay never writes the count that sizes a heap table (`tagpu_replay.c`, `count_kept`), so a puppet would keep whatever its own level init set. An allocation that fails leaves ImpAI unavailable. The work counters are `SIM_PRIVATE`: each level end logs `impai: work seat=<i> ticks=<n> refused=<n> slots_max=<n>/<bound> visits_max=<n>/<bound> cells_max=<n>/<bound> flood_max=<n>/<bound> scans=<n> clusters=<n>`, a tick's largest count against its bound (the slice plus a pass's publish, one observe a slot; the budget plus one walk, which reads each slot's unit once at most; the scan's rows across the map; the flood budget), the patch scan's completed passes and the patches published last. `refused` counts turns whose view or flood arena could not be had (a bound of `tagpu_worldq_open` failed).

## The self-checks

Under `tagpu_impai_check.on` (`SIM_PRIVATE` counts, logged at the level end):

- **Line of sight**, in every ImpAI turn: the rule against the engine's `0x465AC0` for every live unit of another owner that is not submerged, at one instant on one thread. Under True line of sight for the seat's own record (`impai: check los seat=<i> evaluated=<n> mismatch=<n>`); in the other modes, where `0x465AC0` reads the viewed player's bit whatever record it is given, for the local record `main[0x2A43]`, where both read the same bit (`impai: check los-local …`).
- **The seat's site test**, in every ImpAI turn: four sites of the level's smallest plain building type, two near the seat's first unit and two anywhere, tested through the thunk (`WQ_SITE_SEAT`) and held to the seat's own mapped bit at the block `0x47D2E0` reads (`tagpu_worldq_site_mapped`); the same site through the stock test counts where the two answers differ (`impai: check site evaluated=<n> mismatch=<n> accepted=<n> stock_differs=<n>`).
- **Radar**, at `0x46556D`: the engine's pass for the viewed record, then ImpAI's copy for the same record, unbudgeted, and every live unit's bits 8 and 9 compared — bit 10 too where loop 1 cleared it — and each set bit's record held to the unit's incarnation and block of now, which a pass in one call must give, but for the seat's own units, whose record holds the incarnation alone (`impai: check radar passes=<n> evaluated=<n> mismatch=<n> stale=<n>`; the first mismatch's line says `record=0` when the record was the one that differed). Under the check lever every record takes the GameTime of its write (`WQ_HITS.stamp`, `WQ_RADAR_HIT.at`), and each ImpAI seat's publish counts the set bits whose record is older than its pass's start: `stale`, which must be 0, is that count over every seat's publishes, the test that every set bit has its own pass's record. A slot reused while a pass is open is the brain test's case (`check_radar_reuse`, *The brain*).

- **The patches' evidence**, at the level end: each observing seat's published patches (`impai: patch seat=<i> ax az x0 z0 x1 z1 cells`) and the map's metal as the patch rule reads it -- the feature grid's byte `+7` of every cell and the threshold game state `+0xD30` gives the open, held to 255 -- run-length coded (`impai: metal ...`). The first mismatch of each is logged with its slot. `tools/impai/impai_check.py logs --los true|permanent [--puppet <log>]... [--patches] <logs>` judges a game's lines: (a) every check `mismatch=0`, the radar line's `stale=0`, at least 10 000 line-of-sight comparisons (each seat's under True, the local record's under Permanent) and 50 radar passes; (b) with the puppet's logs, the knowledge and overlay digests by GameTime from the `impai: known` lines: on every GameTime both games logged, the same seats with the same pair of digests, at least 20 such GameTimes; one overlay digest for each seat's knowledge digest across both games; between the later of the two games' first such GameTimes and the earlier of their last, no GameTime one game logged and the other did not; and every drawn overlay's line carrying its knowledge's GameTime digest (*The overlay*); (c) every `work` line within its bounds with `refused=0` and at least one completed patch scan; (d) a `cost` line for every seat; (e) every published patch is one the patch rule finds on the whole map's metal of the same level end (each level's patch lines precede its own dump; the logs are read in the order of their parts), recomputed with every cell explored (a closed patch's flood saw every cell it needed explored, so it is the whole map's), and with `--patches` at least one was published.

Without the lever too, each ImpAI seat logs once a level `impai: econ seat=<i> t=<n> pass=<p> metal energy metal_max energy_max metal_in energy_in metal_out energy_out`: what the economy op reads of its record, at its first decision tick from GameTime 590 (`GameTime % 15 == 7·seat % 15`) at which the record's next 30-tick block is not due in that step (`pass`, the record's `+0xF0`, above GameTime). ## The seat's own site test

`0x47D2E0` refuses a site whose footprint centre is unmapped, and the mapped bit it tests is the **viewed** player's, `1 << main+0x2A43` (`mov cl,[ebx+0x2A43]` at `0x47D3D1`), whatever record it is passed ([orders](../engine/orders.html), `0x47D2E0`). ImpAI calls it through a thunk in its stubs' page — four `push [esp+0x10]` and `call 0x47D2E0`, `ret 0x10` — and `0x47D3D1` jumps to a stub that compares the function's return address `[esp+0x3C]` (`sub esp,0x2C` and four pushes in, `0x47D2E0..0x47D2E7`) with the thunk's: equal, it loads the passed record's own bit `[edi+0x146]` (EDI is the record from `0x47D358`); otherwise the stock instruction. Either way it resumes at `0x47D3D7`. Flags need not survive: the `add` at `0x47D3EE` rewrites them before the `je` at `0x47D3FA`. **The invariant is an identity, not a state**: only a call through the thunk returns to it, so every stock caller (`0x40A4CD`, `0x4198C6`, `0x47DBE5`, the only three) reads `main+0x2A43` as before, and no jump lands inside `0x47D3D2..0x47D3D6`. `tagpu_worldq_open(..., WQ_SITE_SEAT, ...)` opens a view whose `site_ok` calls the thunk; it refuses until the install has written every site.

## The brain

`impai_brain_decide` (`impai.h`, the brain) is one decision for one seat over a WORLDQ: engine-free, with no statics, compiled on SSE2 like the shared core. In the game it runs in the seat's turn when `impai_brain_due` holds (`GameTime % 15 == 7·seat % 15`, so no two seats decide on one tick), over the seat's `WQ_SITE_SEAT` view with ImpAI's build menus, the reach labels and the seat's own rows for the threat map (`impai_decide`); between decisions it does no work. Its state is `SIM_STATE`: `s_brain`, one `IMPAI_BRAIN` a seat (the anchor, the fail ring, the explorer), and `s_job`, one `IMPAI_JOB` a unit slot for whichever seat owns it -- a job holds only while its slot holds a live unit of the seat's of the job's type, and a slot reused by the same type inherits it -- both cleared at every level init and a puppet's restored from its recording. Its scratch, and the snap's, are static `SIM_TRANSIENT` buffers written before they are read, so a turn allocates nothing. The level end logs, per seat that decided, `impai: eco seat=<i> decisions orders queued failed army mex energy makers factories cons metal_in energy_in gametime sea_plants built classes ftech yards` (`sea_plants=`, the sea plants the seat ordered over the level; the most units of each category any census counted, frames included -- the most, because a fixture's end scenario clears every unit before the level ends --, and the record's last pass; then what the seat completed over the level, counted by the pulse's walks (*The pulse*): `built=` its units, `classes=` its army units by class as `lraider:4,lassault:2` (`-` none), `ftech=` its factories by tech level as `1:2,2:1`, the last bucket tech 3 and above, and `yards=` its water-only factories) and `impai: work seat=<i> decisions refused census_max=<n>/<block> sites_max snaps_max roots_max queue_max`, each budget's largest use in one decision against the level row's bound, which `impai_check.py` (c) judges. A decision runs, in order: the census over the seat's unit block (counts by category, idle builders by the stock planner's test — no head order or a head with `0x4000` —, claims, unfinished buildings, the factories' queued constructors), the anchor and the base box, the job checks (a job done, failed into the fail ring, or stopped past `job_timeout`), the economy's stalls and projections, the ladder for each idle builder up to `assign_max` (a stall → energy; the opening factory, the least value among the plants whose menu offers a constructor that can build an extractor, a water-only plant (`IMPAI_TF_WATER`) only on a wet anchor as for energy -- CORE's shipyard CORSY, 612.5, is the least of its four plants and fits no site of a dry map; a free patch → extractor, or on a metal map a ring site; energy to keep up; a maker; one explorer; assist), and the factories' constructor queues. Sites come from `impai_build.c`: a patch free of claims and failures, reachable on the builder's class (the reach labels) and under no ground threat, scored in integers and placed by the snap (`tasim_snap_site`) — a constructor explores only when no such patch is left for it —; the base ring (`place_ring` around the base box, `gap` cells apart, off patch metal), which on a **metal map** takes the extractors too: a map whose threshold is 255 (SurfaceMetal, held to 255), so that no cell can stand above it and no patch is ever published, while the loader seeds every cell with the background (`0x4839B3..0x4839E6`, [map](../engine/map.html)), as on Core Prime Industrial Area, every cell 255. That is the stock AI's rule there: it takes its spot search only when SurfaceMetal is below a draw of `0x4B6C30(255)`, 0..254 (`0x40C150..0x40C16C`), and its general search `0x40A5D0` refuses only a site whose footprint holds more than twice the background over its area (`0x40A5EC..0x40A602`, `0x40A76F`) ([orders](../engine/orders.html), *The build site*); how many is the ladder's, as on a patch map. A map with SurfaceMetal between 1 and 254 and no patch is not one: whether a seat has published no patch there depends on what it has explored, so it builds no extractor; the explore cell; the assist. Every site is tested through `site_ok` and every order goes through `can` and `order` within the level row's budgets (the seat's row of `IMPAI_LEVELS`: sites, snaps, roots, queue). `tests/impai_brain_test.py` builds `tests/impai_brain_test.c`, a fake WORLDQ over arrays, natively, under ASan and UBSan, and with the DLL's compiler under Wine, and requires one transcript from all three: the categories and gains, the tech levels (every fake world's from its menus through `impai_menu_union` and the classifier, and a two-side ARM K-bot chain: lab 1, advanced lab 1, a plant a tech-2 constructor builds 2, the advanced lab reached only through the union over the sides), the phases, a stall, a free spot, the opening factory, the caps, the fail ring, sixty decisions of a game in miniature run twice, and **the economy game**: `impai-eco`'s seat 1 on Comet Catcher -- its start and the seven patches within 1100 px of it with their metal (223, 112 or 56 on 3 × 3 cells over a background of 3, read from the game's recording), a CORE commander alone on a 1000/1000 start, the retail game's CORE costs, build times, worker times and gains, and the commander's real plant choices (CORLAB, CORVP and the water-only CORSY with its CORCS and CORUWMEX, which the dry map refuses at every site) -- with a tick-by-tick model of walking, frames growing at workertime / buildtime a second with their cost cut to what the stores hold, an extractor paying extractsmetal times its footprint's metal, a unit with an upkeep working only while the store left by the builders pays it, a factory making constructors, and the brain deciding every 15 ticks to GameTime 5400. ## The military

`impai_mil_decide` (`impai_mil.h`) is the seat's second decision, engine-free and compiled on SSE2 like the
brain. It runs in the seat's turn when `impai_mil_due` holds -- `GameTime % mil_period == (7·seat + 8) % 15`,
every `mil_period` a multiple of 15 -- so it never shares a tick with the seat's economy decision (`7·seat`
mod 15) and a turn costs the larger of the two, not their sum; the two share the turn's scratch (`TURN_SCRATCH`,
the larger of `IMPAI_BRAIN_SCRATCH_MAX` and `IMPAI_MIL_SCRATCH_MAX`). It reads the seat's `WQ_SITE_SEAT` view,
its column of the knowledge rows, the threat table, the type information -- each type's army class
`IMPAI_TINFO.klass`, `impai_class_of` over the same classifier input and output at the content build --, the
economy's anchor and the job table, whose `IMPAI_JOB_SQUAD` jobs name each army unit's squad.

- **Its state** is `SIM_STATE`: `s_mil`, one `IMPAI_MIL` a seat -- the level, the noise seed (the RNG state
  `0x51FC88` read at the level init, never drawn, xor the seat), the table, the picture, sixteen squads (a
  squad's target is a unit slot, the picture's enemy types unit types), the enemy base's estimate, the
  scouting point, the commander's window `safe_until`. Set at a live level's init or for a takeover's fresh seat (`impai_mil_init`): the level
  from `impai_seats_level` (Hard in a network game, else Difficulty `main+0x37EEE`, Normal past 2) or the test lever `tagpu_impai_level.on`
  (`<seat>=easy|normal|hard`); the table `IMPAI_TABLE_DEFAULT` or the file the test lever
  `tagpu_impai_table.on` names (`<seat>=<file>` in the game folder, read at attach, checked by
  `impai_table_load`). It logs `impai: level seat=<i> level=<name> from=lever|difficulty` and `impai: table
  seat=<i> hash=<16 hex> from=lever|default`, and `impai: starts n=<n> <index>:<x>,<z>...`, the level's start
  positions as the `starts` op answers them (*The start positions*, [sim](../engine/sim.html)). Cleared at every
  level init; a puppet's comes from its recording, so a replay plays the recorded level, seed and table, and so does a takeover for every seat ImpAI played there.
- **The weapon scan and the D-gun**: the wrapper runs `0x4089A0(obj, 0)` for an ImpAI seat, a human seat's
  argument, so CommandFire weapons fire only by order, and the military orders the D-gun (resolver mode 4) under
  its three conditions ([the brain](../impai/brain.html), *The military*). Each type's D-gun -- range and energy a
  shot of its longest CommandFire weapon when its def has `candgun`, `+0x245` bit 14 -- is filled into
  `IMPAI_TINFO` at the content build from the classifier's input (`dgun_of`), held to 16 bits. The `mil` lines
  count the D-gun orders (`dgun=`, `dguns=`).
- **The fight content** comes through `impai_fight_content`: the fight tables (*The fight predictor* below), each
  count bounded by its table's capacity, so a count the build did not write yields no content -- every
  prediction refused, no attack wave, no retreat, the commander moved away from every contact worth more than
  it, every pair rate 0 and the factories buying the cheapest type of each class -- and the level's gravity
  `main+0x14263`, read at each decision and held to 1..65536, else 0.
- **Whom a squad attacks** (`fight`, `target_now`, `can_attack`; [the brain](../impai/brain.html), *The
  military*, step 5). A candidate is an enemy row the seat's observation of the same turn still finds as the
  unit the row is about: a row seen in sight must name the type and owner the observation shows (the row's
  owner is an enemy, so whatever unit passes is an enemy of that type the seat sees, whichever incarnation it
  is), and any other row -- a radar contact, a shared one, a row never identified -- the incarnation the
  seat's radar records hold (`IMPAI_VIEW.hits`), as the publish tests a contact; a slot taken since by another
  unit fails. A member is then ordered at a candidate only where the order resolver `0x43F0E0` answers its
  attack case ([orders](../engine/orders.html), *What each resolver mode answers*), its tests read in its order:
  - the unit's side, from its type's facts at the content build (`IMPAI_TINFO.attack`, `attack_of` over the
    def's flags and the role input's weapons 1 and 2) and the census's `+0x110` bit 31;
  - the target's side, `WQ_OBS.aim`: `+0x110 & 3 == 2`, the altitude's and the def top's high words summed
    against the sea byte as `0x43F1F7..0x43F208` does, and the def's canfly. `observe` gives it only where it
    identifies the unit (OWN, LOS): those are the target's type and state, which a radar dot does not show, so a
    radar-only candidate is taken on the unit's own two tests (canattack; a weapon or kamikaze) and the resolver
    decides the rest when the order is asked -- a refusal counted, no order spent.
  - **Premises, not established, so not by construction:** that a unit's slot weapons and weapon 2's
    presence bit `+0x3B & 2` stay as the creates set them -- the slots' setup `0x49E070` (called at
    `0x485F04`, `0x4860AC`, `0x4862C4`) copies the def's three weapons into slots 0..2 (`0x49E0A1..0x49E0B0`)
    and sets each slot's bit `0x2` when that weapon's ID byte is not the null record's 0
    (`0x49E0C2..0x49E0E8`), DISASSEMBLED, while the resolver reads the unit's `+0x10`, `+0x2C` and `+0x3B`
    and the content the def's `weapon1` and `weapon2`, and no search has ruled out a later write of either;
    and that every army unit has a mover, which the non-flying answer `ATTACK_CHASE` needs (`0x43F348`; army
    is mobile and mobile is `bmcode` 1, the creates' own test for one, `0x4860BB`, `0x4862CF`) -- a failed
    mover allocation leaves a type with no mover, no `+0x110` bit 29 and no kamikaze, which the resolver
    refuses. A target pending death (`+0x110` bit 14) this tick is still observed and still refused.
  - **A member with no candidate it can attack keeps with the squad**: a move to the centroid when it stands
    farther than `SPREAD_PX` (64 px) from it, nothing nearer, so it costs no order beside the squad. The
    members are ordered in two passes, the attacks and the steps back first and those moves after, so the
    decision's `mil_roots` go to the fight first (MEASURED by `impai_brain_test`: Easy's six orders all reach
    six tanks' attacks, four idle anti-air K-bots beside or behind the squad taking none).
- **The naval rule** reads the reach labels and tops through `IMPAI_MIL_IN.reach` and the brain's `in.reach`, the
  same view (`reach_view`): the judgement right after the picture, every move through `move_to`, a member's
  attack only on a candidate within its range + 384 or that it can travel to (`target_of`), the sides'
  `closes` from the labels; the sea plant in the economy's ladder ([the brain](../impai/brain.html), *The naval
  rule*). `impai-naval` and its stock-limits twin play it on Gods of War, and the harness's `naval` check passes
  the live test only when a seat's mil line says `naval=1` and its eco line `sea_plants=` at least 1.
- **A prediction's distance is the fronts' gap**, because the predictor tests its ranges against it: at a
  retreat, the squad's member nearest the contact's member nearest the squad's centroid; for the commander, the
  nearest of it and the army beside it to the contact's member nearest it; for an attack wave, the squad's member
  nearest the target point, which stands for a census of no position. The run's ~90 KB of work is the
  decision's (`MDEC.fw`).
- **The pulse**, once a game minute a seat on its economy phase (`GameTime % 1800 == 7·seat % 15`), always on:
  `impai: pulse seat=<i> t=<GameTime> built=<n> mroots=<n> eroots=<n> army=<n> squads=<letters> metal=<x>
  energy=<x> vlive=<x> vbuilt=<x>` -- units completed, the military's and the economy's orders, all since the
  level began; the last military census's army; each squad's task (`F` form, `D` defend, `R` raid, `A` attack,
  `B` back, a retreat, `S` scout, `-` none); TotalMetalProduced `+0xB4` and TotalEnergyProduced `+0xAC`; the
  value of the seat's finished units alive and of its units completed. A unit is counted completed once, at the
  first walk that finds it finished, keyed by its slot's incarnation (`tagpu_unit_birth`; without the fail-closed
  table, by its type, so a slot's next unit of the same type is not counted); the walks run at each decision and
  at the pulse, so a unit finished and destroyed between two of them is not counted either. The bar's stall
  rule reads it ([the bar](../impai/bar.html)).
- **The level end** logs, per seat that decided, `impai: mil seat=<i> level decisions refused predictions
  refused_predictions roots refused_orders left_out attacks raids retreats scouts defends safe dguns
  army_max=<n>/512 enemies_max=<n>/2048 contacts_max=<n>/32 predict_max=<n>/<row> roots_max=<n>/<row> naval
  unreached cross tech_m` -- `naval=` the last judgement (-1 never judged), `unreached=` the moves given no order
  because no block of the member's component lay within 32 blocks of the point, `cross=` the predictions with a
  side that cannot close ([the brain](../impai/brain.html), *The naval rule*), `tech_m=` the metal income a pass
  at which the seat techs up, the table's `tech_m` times the level row's `tech_scale` -- and a decision that
  begins a task logs `impai: mil seat=<i> t=<t> attack raid scout retreat safe army`. The `eco` line counts
  the army units the factories queued (`army=`).

## The fight predictor

`src/impai_fight.c` (`impai_fight.h`) predicts a fight between two sides -- up to 16 types each, a count and a
health fraction a type, the gap between the two sides' fronts (their nearest unit centres) and whether each side
closes -- as a stepped fight of volleys over the 5400-tick horizon of the references: 60 steps of 30 ticks, then
40 of 90, ended when a side is gone or nothing moves and nothing is in reach, and scored where it stands. Its
engine facts, each with its addresses in the file's header ([weapons](../engine/weapons.html)):
- **The range test is flat and centre to centre** (can-engage `0x49ABB0`), and a ballistic weapon's reach is
  `min(range, v² / (g · 65536))` with g the level's gravity, from the solver `0x49A890`.
- **Each type closes at its own maxvelocity** until a live enemy type it can hurt is within its **first armed
  slot's** reach less a 32-px stand-off, whether or not that slot hurts it (`chase_reach`): Attack_Chase takes its
  slot from `0x4897E0` -- the first of slots 0..2 whose flag byte (unit `+0x1F`, `+0x3B`, `+0x57`) has bit `0x2`,
  and slot 0 when none has it; slots 3..9 are never its -- stores it at `0x4035A1`, walks toward the target (that
  slot's range read at `0x403623`) and stops on the first tick that slot's can-engage passes (`0x4035D6`). The
  slots' setup sets bit `0x2` when the slot's weapon has an ID byte `+0x10A` other than the null record's 0
  (`0x49E0C2..0x49E0E8`), so any weapon arms its slot, a stockpile or interceptor one included. DISASSEMBLED.
  So ARMAMPH (260 against 700), CORKROG (300, 900), ARMPT (180, 604) and CORPT (180, 590) walk in to their first
  weapon, and a type whose weapons all sit past slot 2 walks to contact. The 32 is [INFERRED], fitted against 0,
  16 and 48; a type that can hurt nothing walks to contact. **Not modelled:** the content leaves stockpile and
  interceptor weapons out, so a slot holding one reads as empty and its type closes to its next armed slot's
  reach, where the engine's chase walks to the left-out weapon's.
- **Each weapon of each type fires volleys**: loaded at the start (the slot is zeroed at creation), counting
  down whether or not it has a target, and reloading for `⌊(120 − ⌊20 hp/maxhp⌋) · reload / 100⌋` ticks
  (`0x49E48D..0x49E4EE`, veterancy left at 0); its hits spread over the types in its reach, (1 − focus) by
  count and focus on the quickest kill.
- **A hit deals at most one unit's hp** (the apply `0x489CE0`), and an area hit's splash -- its area over the
  target's spacing, squared, at the falloff's mean over a disc -- reaches more units, each of which can die.
- **The hit probability** is `s(bias + lead · l)` by the weapon's kind: STRAIGHT (beams, guns, unguided rockets,
  missiles guided to a point), BALLISTIC, HOMING (`selfprop ∧ ((guidance ∧ ¬twophase) ∨ (twophase ∧ tracks))`,
  what the projectile tick steers onto a moving unit: torpedoes, CORRL's missile) and DROPPED. l is 0 on a
  target standing and, on one moving at its maxvelocity, its travel over the shot's flight at its reach over
  the radius a blast can land within and still hurt it, `(size + aoe)/2`, or for a homing shot the target's
  speed over the shot's.
It answers each side's kept value share, the margin `m` (side A's less side B's) and `P(A wins) = s(m / tau)`,
from the 11 coefficients of `IMPAI_FIT` (`src/impai_fitparams.c`: a bias and a lead a kind, focus, splash, tau).
Not modelled: terrain and height, armour (COB state), veterancy, minimum range, turning and aiming, accuracy and
spray, the engine's own target choice, each type's braking, and a unit idling once its one target dies.
`impai_fight_rate_of` is the buy's `IMPAI_PREDICTOR.rate` -- a type's damage a tick on a target moving at its
maxvelocity, volleys spread over the reload -- and `impai_expf` its `expf`, defined here once. Engine-free C on
SSE2 with `-ffp-contract=off` and no libm transcendental, its structs fixed-width (`IMPAI_FTYPE` 40 bytes,
`IMPAI_FWEAPON` 36, `IMPAI_FDMG` 8, `IMPAI_FIT` 44) and the run's work the caller's (`IMPAI_FWORK`, 90 392 bytes
on both targets), so the DLL, the native test builds and the fit's library (`tools/impai/fightlib.c`, built by
`tools/tasim/native.py fightlib`) compute the same bits; `tests/impai_fight_test.py` requires one transcript from
all three builds (mirror fights draw, a swap negates `m`, a doubled army never loses, refusals, the kind table on
the engine's flag words, the first volley, the reach cap, the chase walking to its first armed slot of slots 0..2
alone, a NaN coefficient killing nothing, `impai_expf` within 2^-22, the merge); a NaN reaching a pool or a
clamp is taken as 0, never converted to an integer. **The cost**,
MEASURED 2026-11-13: 16 types of 3000 units a side over the whole horizon take 123 µs native and 146 µs in the
i686 build under Wine, 8 types 42 µs, 4 types 15-20 µs, and sides out of reach that do not close 16-22 µs; a
military decision makes at most 16 predictions (`IMPAI_PREDICT_CAP`), so at most about 2.3 ms.

**The fight content** is built at the level init beside the threat table, all or none with the menus: the
filler `tagpu_worldq_fight_in` (`tagpu_worldq.c`, beside the role classifier's input) reads each type's hp
`+0x1FA`, speed `+0x192`, footprint, waterline `+0x22C`, model top `+0x16E` and weapons, and each weapon's
range, velocity, reload, burst, damage, area, edge, accuracy, spray and flags and its `[DAMAGE]` rows resolved to
types, every count bounded by its table (`IMPAI_FWEAPONS` 4096, `IMPAI_FDMG_POOL` 65536); a weapon's kind byte is
`impai_fight_kind` of its flags. A mod whose counts do not fit has no fight content and so, the content being all
or none, no ImpAI seat; the line `impai: fight none: the engine's types, weapons or [DAMAGE] rows do not fit the
tables (…)` says so, and the compatibility suite's ImpAI stage and `netai_check.py` fail on it by name
([the suite](../compat/suite.html), *The ImpAI stage*). **How far from the pool the mods are:** the retail game
logs `types=279 weapons=200 dmgrows=653` (MEASURED, every ImpAI level init logged since 2026-11-10). The build
cannot run outside the game -- it reads the engine's loaded tables -- and the offline loader (`tasim_run defs`
over a setup's files) bounds it only where it reads the mod as the engine does: every [DAMAGE] key of every
weapon but a stockpile or interceptor one, an upper bound while unit names are unique, is 688 for retail and
7763 for TA Twilight 2.0b98 (505 units, 250 weapons, MEASURED 2026-11-13). It reads Total Mayhem's, ProTA's,
Escalation's and TA Zero's weapons as retail's 198 (and Escalation's and TA Zero's units as retail's 278), so it
says nothing of those four; their figure is the `dmgrows=` of their next compatibility run. A type is
`SUBMERGED` when its top is below the sea at rest (y + top < sea, `0x465AC0`'s test; at rest a sea unit
stands at the sea less its waterline [INFERRED]); `klass` is `impai_class_of`'s. The tables are `SIM_CONTENT`
(`s_ftype`, `s_fweapon`, `s_fdmg`, their counts and layouts); the level init logs `impai: fight types= weapons=
dmgrows= at=` and, under the check lever, their FNV-1a 64 as `impai: fight digest=`.

**The fit** is `tools/impai/predfit.py` against the engine's own fights ([calibration](../impai/calibration.html)),
the calibration references of `duelgen.py`'s duels, groups, defence and naval fights ([tasim
calibration](tasim-calibration.html)), split into a training and a check half by the FNV-1a 32 of the
scenario's stem:
- **`content`** peeks the three tables out of a held GDI launch and checks them against the digest and `tasim_run
  defs` (type 0, the reserved `None`, has no files), writing the file (`impai-fight-content/2`) only on PASS.
  Each fight's gravity comes from its reference's map in `.data/impai/map-gravity.json` (`{"maps": {name:
  main+0x14263}}`); a reference on a map the table lacks is refused by name.
- **The loss** is the binomial loss plus 4 n (m − M)² over the training half, plus the prior's pull on each free
  coefficient's squared distance from the start (bias 2, lead −1, focus 0.5, splash 1, tau 0.2), in a box that
  holds every lead at or below 0. It is a staircase in the coefficients -- a unit dies whole, so the loss jumps
  where a coefficient crosses a kill -- so an unpulled search over-fits: it finds a deeper step, not a better
  model.
- **Held by design**: the homing kind's bias 2 and lead −1. A guided shot at a standing target hits as a direct
  one does, and a target fleeing at a fifth of the shot's speed is still caught about 86 % of the time
  (`s(2 − 0.2)`) [INFERRED: a radial-flight bound]; on the 117 decisive training fights of the one-order
  references (`ceed681e6fa9`) the homing bias moved 25 margins and the lead 7, flipping none, and holding them cost
  nothing in the cross-validation. The dropped
  kind moves no fight and is
  held by the free test.
- **`cv`** chooses the prior and the held set by 5-fold cross-validation inside the training half, stratified by
  kind and repeated 4 times, each fold fit the best of 3 seeds: the candidate of the lowest summed validation
  data loss among priors 30 to 3000 (extended threefold past an end the choice sits on) and the homing hold with
  or without splash held at 1. The accuracy is printed beside it and never chooses. The folds run on every core
  at nice 19 against a private copy of the library, about 4 minutes. `fit` runs the choice on the whole training
  half with 6 seeds, refusing a cross-validation of another reference set, and records its table and estimate;
  `emit` writes `impai_fitparams.c` (`--start` reproduces the starting point). `fit`, `cv` and `selftest` refuse a
  check-half fight.
- **`check`** reads the check half once, after the fit: the 90 % rule on its decisive fights (at least 40), and
  the same 90 % for each kind with at least 20 decisive check-half fights; a kind with fewer is reported as too
  few to judge. Each reference is judged on its own arena, the water arena for a naval fight and the land arena
  for the rest, and one played elsewhere is refused by name. **Limit:** the water arena is the default one,
  `calib-arena-naval.json`.
- **`selftest`** fits fights the model made at known coefficients, at prior 1 with nothing held, and requires the
  truth's loss within 2 %, its margins within 0.05 and the check (at prior 0 the search stops on a step 3.7 %
  above the truth's loss).

**The fitted coefficients** (`impai_fitparams.c`, MEASURED 2026-11-13) are fitted to the training half of the
re-targeting references, reference set `defcfcdb2359` (`.data/calib-rt/refs`: 284 graded fights, defence 30,
duel 64, group 150, naval 40; 142 training fights, 121 of them decisive), content `CA6CC97C978BF7DF`. The
cross-validation chooses **prior 300 with homing held** (validation loss 14047; the neighbours 14252 at 100 and
14800 at 1000), and the final fit, best of 6 seeds that agree to 0.12 of a loss of 3096, keeps straight bias
2.07 and lead −0.74, ballistic bias 1.67 and lead −1.63, focus 0.27, splash 0.81 and tau 0.231; homing stays
at (2, −1) and dropped, which no training fight fires, at its start.

**The honest estimate** is the cross-validation's accuracy on unseen training-half fights: **428/484 (88.4 %,
95 % CI 82-93 % on the 121 unique fights)**: defence 44/48, duel 76/80, **group 248/288 (86.1 %, 76-92 %)**,
naval 60/68 (88.2 %); by class, 416/464 (89.7 %) of the fights that end before the limit, 8/16 of the 4 long ones
and 4/4 of the one stalemate. The re-targeting protocol emptied the classes the one-order references stalled in
(116 of the 121 decisive training fights end, against 79 of 117), so the estimate now rests on fights that end,
and those score lower than the one-order references' did (296/316): the fights that end are no longer the easy
ones. Group, the kind ImpAI decides most, is below 90 % in the cross-validation at every candidate (85.8 % to 88.9 %).

**The check** (MEASURED 2026-11-13, run once on the check half after the fit): **PASS, 113/122 (92.6 %)** of the
decisive check-half fights; group 64/69 (92.8 %), the one kind with 20 or more decisive fights, passes its own 90 %;
defence 13/15, duel 19/19 and naval 17/19 are too few to judge. The training half scores 109/121 (90.1 %) and all
284 fights 222/243 (91.4 %). The nine fights it gets wrong: defence-001 and -021, group-017, -020, -042, -062 and
-080, naval-012 and -021. The check's 92.6 % sits above the cross-validation's 88.4 % and inside its interval; the
cross-validation is the estimate to plan with, and with group under 90 % there it is no margin. The check half
is not clean of the design: the re-targeting protocol was diagnosed on two of its fights, group-141 and naval-012
([decisions](../impai/decisions.html), *The re-targeting corpus*); naval-012 is still one it gets wrong.

## The overlay

The test lever `tagpu_impaiov.on`, `<seat>[:<layer>]` with the layer `ground`, `air`, `water`, `influence`, `presence` or `reach` (default `ground`), shows what one seat knows; `tacli arm <instance> impaiov.on=1:air` writes it. Never a player setting. A seat shows while ImpAI plays it or its knowledge generation is above 0, so a puppet shows the seat its recording played.

- **The fill** (`tagpu_impai_overlay`), from the frame packet's publisher on the game thread: one `PK_IMPAIHDR` (seat, layer, radar age = GameTime less the last published pass, knowledge generation) and at most 16 384 `PK_IMPAI` of 16 bytes (`TAGPU_PK_TRUNC_IMPAI` when cut) -- the layer's nonzero 256-px cells, shaded 1 + floor(log2 value) held to 15 (influence carries its sign; reach the label of each cell's centre block for the level's lowest movement class), then every row in slot order (own, contact, ghost, with its classes), then the published patches. It reads ImpAI's declared state and content, and of the engine only what the recording carries too -- GameTime, the map's size, the seat's alliance row -- and the ground's height `0x485070` at each point; so a puppet's overlay is the live game's.
- **The maps' cache** (`SIM_PRIVATE`, about 459 KB): rebuilt in the first fill of each 30-tick epoch (GameTime / 30) in which the knowledge generation moved, and in a level's first fill; the level init forgets it. Rebuilt too in a fill whose GameTime or knowledge generation is below the build's: in play both only grow, so that is a restore (a seek, a rewind, a takeover's), and a restore into the same epoch shows the restored state's maps. A fill runs only when the render thread has taken the last packet (the publisher's FRESH gate), so the rebuilds' GameTimes follow the renderer's pace and differ between a live game and its puppet.
- **The draw** (`tagpu_mark.c`, `gather_impai`), after the reclaim sweep, fog off: a cell as a filled square centred in it, its side growing with the shade; a row as a 4-px square -- own blue, line of sight red, shared radar cyan, radar or sonar yellow, a ghost grey; a patch as a green ring; the readout `IMPAI SEAT 1 - GROUND - RADAR AGE 12` in white. A cell takes its layer's colour -- ground red, air cyan, water blue, influence green (red where negative), presence yellow --, and a reach cell cycles its label through 249..254. The colours are the standard palette's system entries 248..255 (grey, red, green, yellow, blue, magenta, cyan, white).
- **The digests**, under the check lever. `tagpu_impai_frame` logs `impai: known seat=<i> tick=<t> known=<FNV of the seat's rows and patches> ov=<FNV of the maps, the reach cells and the items> items=<n>` once at every GameTime that is a multiple of 30, for each seat ImpAI plays or whose knowledge generation is above 0. The overlay digest is built at that GameTime from that knowledge: the same build and items as the drawn overlay's (`ov_build`, `ov_items`, the overlay lever's layer), into scratch the call frees, so neither the drawn overlay's cache nor the renderer's pace reaches it; `ov=none` when the map is past the maps' grid or the scratch could not be had. It runs at the end of every in-play draw (the publisher's `after_draw`, outside the publish's gate), the one point both games pass after each step: a live game's knowledge is what its observation made in the step, and a puppet's is the recording's, put by the frame visit before the draw. A puppet logs a GameTime only when that frame's visit put the recording at it (`tagpu_replay_put_at`); at the level's last step, which has no image after it, and while a seek, a hold or a rewind runs, the visit puts nothing, so a puppet's GameTime is exact or missing. Under the harness's turbo at k = 1 a frame runs at most one step, so both games log the same GameTimes. At stock pacing a frame can run up to five steps, and a GameTime it steps past is missing from that game's log. The work runs only under the check lever, in the draw, outside the turn the cost line times. Each rebuild of the drawn overlay's cache also logs `impai: ov seat=<i> tick=<t> known=<FNV> ov=<FNV> items=<n>`, at the renderer's pace. `impai_check.py` (b) pairs a live game's `known` lines with its puppet's by GameTime: on every GameTime both logged, the same seats with the same pair of digests, at least 20 such GameTimes; across both games one seat's knowledge digest maps to one overlay digest, never `none`; and between the later first and the earlier last of the two games' GameTimes neither logged one the other did not. An `ov` line whose knowledge a GameTime logged must carry that GameTime's overlay digest, which holds the drawn overlay to the one built at the GameTime; the `ov` lines are never counted.

## The cost

`QueryPerformanceCounter` around each turn -- the views' opens, the knowledge tick and the decision, not the self-checks nor the weapon scan -- kept per seat as the largest, the sum and a log2 histogram of microseconds (`SIM_PRIVATE`); the level end logs `impai: cost seat=<i> ticks=<n> max_us=<n> mean_us=<x> p99_us=<n> slice=<n> visits=<n> nslots=<n> hist=<edge>:<turns>,...`, p99 and each histogram bucket by its upper edge in microseconds. **The tail by cause**: each turn is classed by what it did -- `publish` (it ended a radar pass and published its rows), `scan` (the patch scan's last row), `decide` (a decision), `plain` (none of them), a turn counted in each class it belongs to -- and the level end logs `impai: tail seat=<i> over_us=1000 publish=<slow>/<turns>:<max>:<mean> scan=… decide=… plain=… worst_us=<n> gametime=<t> publish= scan= decide= slots= visits= cells= flood=`, the slowest turn's counts whole. A slow `plain` turn did only its slice and a bounded radar step, so its time is the OS's (preemption, a fault), not its work's. A measurement, never a verdict: the gate judges the counts of the `work` line. The test lever `tagpu_impai_budget.on` raises the slice to the design point's 15 001 slots and the radar pass to 10^6 visits a tick, to measure each loop's cost per unit of work. The measured figures and the budgets they set: [ImpAI for the player](../impai/player.html), *Cost*.

## The seat table

`s_seat[10]` is `SIM_STATE`, keyed by player record.

- **The level init** (`tagpu_impai_level_init`), on the loader thread inside `0x4919C8`, right after the engine's players init `0x464990`, clears the table on every level, live or puppet. On a live level it reads each record into the engine-free rule `impai_seats_pick` (`impai_seats.c`, three-build tested by `tests/impai_seats_test.py`). **Outside a network game** (`main+0x2A44` bit 0 clear) it marks each record that is seated with controller 2, the AI object `+0x74` set and its `seatai` bit set, logging `impai: seats 1,2 (impure.cfg) of 4 records` (or `seats none`); a record with controller 3 there marks none and logs `impai: seats none: record N is remote outside a network game`. **In a network game** it marks each such record whose long name `+0x2B` begins `ImpAI:` (*The network choice*), and only a record of controller 2 with the AI object can be marked: the engine makes a computer seat type 2 with its AI object only on the peer that added it, and a type-3 record with no AI object on every other peer, where the players phase never calls `0x465031` for it ([network](../engine/network.html), *A computer seat in a network game*). So each peer marks only seats it runs, by its own store, and no row index crosses the wire — rows can differ between peers. It logs `impai: seats 2 (netai=impai, a network game, level 2) of 4 records; other peers' AI seats 3, ImpAI's 3`: the records marked, the store's first step, the level, the records seated, the type-3 records whose PlayerInfo `+0x94` is 2 — the AI seats another peer runs, which this peer never marks — and those of them whose names say that peer's ImpAI plays them. **The level** (`impai_seats_level`) is the Difficulty `main+0x37EEE` in a skirmish (a value past 2 plays Normal, and the military's init says so) and **Hard in every network game**: the host's Start stores 2 and a joiner's value is whatever its last screen, saved game or registry left ([sim](../engine/sim.html), *Difficulty and the AI handicap*), so Hard is the one level the game set and every peer agrees on. Each marked seat's military takes it at the level init (`impai_mil_init`: `impai: level seat=<i> level=<name> from=difficulty`, or `from=lever` under the level lever), and with a seat marked the init logs the map's start positions (`impai: starts n=…`). On a puppet level it marks nothing: the recording restores the table at the level's first step, on the game thread, and between the two clears the recorder is its only writer. A recording that carries no row of the table leaves it clear.
- **A takeover** (`tagpu_impai_takeover`, from `tagpu_replay.c`'s `tk_end`) marks ImpAI's seats: the takeover makes every recorded seat but the player's a fresh stock AI (new AI objects in TK1) and the player's a human's (type 1, `tk_types`), and ImpAI's seats are the ones it played in the recording — the marks R13 restored, read before the table is written — plus any the playing process's `seatai` adds, through the level init's rule, which marks only a seated controller-2 record with its AI object. So the player's seat is never ImpAI's, and no other seat ImpAI played loses it, whatever store the playing process has. A seat ImpAI played in the recording and still plays **keeps** what R13 restored -- its knowledge, brain, jobs and military with the recorded level, seed and table -- so the fork goes on from the recorded state, as a rewind's does, and for every kept seat its first capture holds the parent's tables at the fork, which `tarec compare-fork` compares. A seat ImpAI did not play there starts fresh (`seat_fresh`): its columns and rows zero, its brain reset, the jobs of its unit block cleared, a new military (`impai_mil_init`), so its mark, brain, jobs and military differ from the parent's by design, and `compare-fork` leaves them out at the fork's first capture only: `s_seat`, `s_brain` and `s_mil`'s rows of the seat and `s_job`'s rows of its unit block, for each seat the fork's header marks ImpAI's (`RT_PLAYER` byte 15) and the parent segment's header does not (`tarec.py`, `fork_view`'s (e), the owner's decision of 2026-11-13 in [decisions](../impai/decisions.html)). Every later capture is compared in full, and so is every other seat's ImpAI state at the first. Its knowledge tables need no mask: no turn writes them for a seat ImpAI does not play and the level init clears them to zero, which `seat_fresh` writes. A seat marked in the parent's header that the AI command cleared before the fork and `seatai` names again is fresh to the takeover but not to the header rule, so `compare-fork` names its rows; no test group plays that case. `impai-takeover-fresh` and its stock twin play a fresh seat (*The replay features and their tests*). A seat ImpAI no longer plays keeps its rows, which no turn reads, and loses its knowledge generation (`s_seatst.known_gen`), which the overlay and the digests' log test for a seat to show, so its frozen knowledge is not drawn. The content is the puppet level init's, so the takeover builds none; a level without it marks no seat. It runs on the game thread inside the takeover's frame visit, after the puppet mode has ended and after R13 has written the recording's ImpAI tables, which would otherwise overwrite the marks, and before the fork is armed, so the fork's first capture and its header's ImpAI byte state the new marks. Its line is `impai: seats 1 (the recording's and impure.cfg's, a takeover) of 3 records; fresh none`. A rewind's fork keeps the saved present's marks and skips it.
- **The level end** (`tagpu_impai_level_end`), on the game thread in the teardown `0x491B60`'s entry, after the recorder has closed the level's recording: it logs `impai: calls seat=<i> n=<turns> gametime=<t>` for each marked seat, `impai: handicap rows scaled/exempt gametime=<t>: 1=<s>/<e> … 18=<s>/<e>` (the rows with a site: 15 and 16 are absent), the observation's `work`, `cost`, `tail` and `check` lines (*What a seat knows*, *The cost*) and the brain's `eco` and `work` lines (*The brain*), and **clears the table**. So nothing between two levels reads a mark: the next load's loader thread, the only thread that can reach a site before that load's level init (its early pump `0x49727D`, in a network load), is created afterwards by the game thread (`0x4982CA`).
- **Why a game-thread reader sees the whole table**: the level init runs before the loader's last store `0x497C62`, and the game thread runs no step until it has read that store's bit (`0x498342`), the ordering `tagpu_replay.c`'s *THE VIEWER'S BUFFERS* rests on.
- **The recording's header** says which AI played each seat: `RT_PLAYER`'s byte 15 is `tagpu_impai_seat_ai(i)`, 1 when the record is active, controller 2 and marked, else 0 — also in a recording made before the seat choice. The reader refuses a byte above 1. It is written at the header's first capture, after the level init, or at a fork's after the takeover's restore.

## The handicap exemption

Fifteen of the seventeen Difficulty type tests and the on-hit commander purge `0x406FC3` ([sim](../engine/sim.html), *Difficulty and the AI handicap*, has the table with each site's unscaled branch U and scaled continuation S) each become a 5-byte `jmp` to a stub of its own (the rest of the site NOPs). A stub:

1. runs the site's own compare, copied from the site, and its `jne` to U, so a record stock does not scale is not scaled;
2. for a type-2 record, bounds it as the seam does: an offset from record 0 below `0xCEE` (the engine's own loop bound, `0x46602D`) that divides by `0x14B`;
3. when the seat table marks the record, increments the row's exempt count and jumps to U; else increments its scaled count and jumps to S.

It restores `EAX`, `ECX` and `EDX`, touches no x87 register (zero to two values are live on the FPU stack at a site; none at rows 13, 14 and 18: `0x464B88` and `0x464CB8` empty it with `fstp st(0)` before a call, and `0x406F80` holds no x87 value) and calls nothing; the `inc` writes only the flags, which are dead at all 32 targets. Seat 2 marked and seat 1 not, a scaled count is an OTA AI seat's and an exempt one an ImpAI seat's. The counts (`s_diag.hits[18][2]`, a pair a row id, so rows 15 and 16 stay 0) are `SIM_PRIVATE` diagnostics, cleared at the level init; nothing in the simulation reads them.

**Rows 15 and 16, the Deathmatch respawn's two grants, stay stock** (`0x465437`, `0x4654A3`): no ImpAI seat can reach them. The respawn `0x4653D9` creates its commander for the controlled player only: the owner its create `0x485F50` is given is the byte `main+0x2A42`, pushed at `0x4653D2` (DISASSEMBLED; [network](../engine/network.html), *The Deathmatch respawn*), and the type test reads that unit's owner. The controlled player is the local human's record, type 1, which the level init never marks (it marks computer seats, controller 2); it becomes type 2 only through the `AI` command, which clears its mark (below). So the exempt branch could never be taken, and the sites are left as stock has them; the row ids keep the numbering of the [sim](../engine/sim.html) table. A stock respawn needs `ActiveCommanderDeath` 2, which only the multiplayer battle room sets.

**The ordering.** The table is written by the level init on the loader thread inside `0x4919C8`, before every loader-thread path that reaches a site after it (`0x497B29`, `0x497B40`, `0x497C10`) and before the game thread's first step; then only by the recording's restore and the `AI` command, both on the game thread between steps, and by the level end's clear. The one path before the level init, the loader's early pump in a network load (a removed player's units destroyed: scout I1 of the ImpAI plan, function-level reachability), reads the table the previous level's end cleared, so every type-2 record takes S there. On a puppet every recorded record is type 3, which the site's own compare sends to U as stock does.

**Writing `main+0x37EEE` around calls instead is rejected**: the sites are reached from the economy pass, transfers (including the network's), reclaim, building and unit death, and no one bracket covers them.

## The `AI` command

`0x416280`, the console's `AI` (row `0x501FD0`, mask 4: cheats), sets the type of the active record its argument names through `0x463C60` at one of two calls: type 1 becomes 2 at `0x4162F4` (`push 2`), types 2 and 3 become 1 at `0x4162FE` (`push 1`). Both calls come to `impai_ai_command`, `__fastcall(rec, edx, type)` (the record in `ECX`, the type on the stack, popped by the callee as `0x463C60`'s `ret 4` does), which clears the record's mark, bounded as above and never on a puppet, logs `impai: the AI command makes record <i> type <t>: ImpAI no longer plays it` when there was one, and calls `0x463C60`. So `AI` never leaves a seat to ImpAI: on an ImpAI seat it makes the seat a human's, and a second `AI` hands it to the stock AI; on a human's seat it hands it to the stock AI, as in stock, even one a takeover played as an ImpAI seat. It runs on the game thread, or on the loader thread through a profile script line in `0x4648E0`, inside the players init `0x464990`, before the level init clears the table and marks the record if `seatai` names it ([recorder](../engine/recorder.html), the `AI` command's entry).

## The renderer

The AI's test games render with GDI and take no GPU token. `tools/impai/bar.py play`,
`tourney.py play` (and the `tourney.py play` commands `tune.py tierB next` and `tune.py plan`
write), `eco_bench.py engine` and `tools/tasim/calib.py engine` launch every lane with
`tagpu_gdi.on` in its game folder (tareplay's `Worker.gdi`): the DLL reads the lever at attach and
picks the GDI backend, which makes no Vulkan device (`tagpu_cfg.c`; the log says `cfg: ...
renderer GDI (tagpu_gdi.on)`), and each tool holds the run lock with `gpu=False`. `--gpu` on each
renders on the default renderer, for a debugging run, and takes a GPU token a lane.
`tools/replay/tareplay.py run --gdi` plays the ImpAI test groups (`impai-eco`, `impai-fight`,
`impai-handicap`, `impai-naval`, `impai-observe*`) the same way during development: one run's record, puppets,
round trips and seeks share the renderer, so no comparison it makes crosses two, and its summary's
first line names the renderer. `tareplay.py landing` and `broad` play the players' renderer.
`handicap_check.py` and `econ_check.py` launch their caller's instance with `tagpu_gdi.on` as well,
leaving the lever as they found it, and hold the lock with `gpu=False` (`--gpu` as above);
`handicap_row6.py` writes it into the instance it makes, `l1_run.py` and `tools/tasim/applysweep.py
run` set `Worker.gdi`, and `netai_check.py`, `netroom_check.py` and `netprobe.py` write it into both
peers' game folders (`twopeers.py`), each holding the lock with `gpu=False` and taking `--gpu` as
above. `l1_run.py`'s `run.txt`, `applysweep.json`, `netprobe.py`'s `probe.json` and the network
checks' `renderer.txt` name the renderer.

**A result is compared only with results of its own renderer.** Each bar, tourney and eco-bench
result and each calibration lane-launch names its renderer (one written before the field is the
default renderer's); the bar's, tourney's and eco bench's judgements and `calib.py refs build`
refuse a set that mixes two, and `bar.py play`, `tourney.py play` and `calib.py engine --resume`
keep only results of the renderer asked for. GDI is the ground truth for the AI's tests: results
made on the default renderer (A2a's calibration among them) are not compared with GDI ones until
they are made again under GDI.

**Whether the renderer changes the simulation.** Under the Vulkan backend the game thread skips
engine draws that GDI runs (`tagpu_owndraw.c`, `tagpu_fxown.c`, `tagpu_featown.c`,
`tagpu_terrown.c`, each armed by the render thread's own pass), and that those draws write no
simulation state is argued per site, not checked. **On the exact scenarios it does not:**
`tools/tasim/rendereq.py` played `exact-damage-armllt` three times with GDI and three with `--gpu`
and its 108 calibration trace lines were equal on every run (MEASURED 2026-11-13, `rendereq: the
renderers agree (3 GDI runs, 3 Vulkan runs)`), so GDI-made references hold for players on the
Vulkan renderer there. The image digest differed at GameTime 0, in the player records or the
projectile pool; the tool reports that as information, since the trace is what a reference grades.
Calibration's start check R4 sees the scoreboard rank, player record `+0x148`, move between runs
under GDI (MEASURED 2026-11-13: 32 runs) and not in the earlier Vulkan runs; its one writer in play
outside a network game is the scoreboard `0x4948E0`, whose one caller is DrawGameScreen
([units](../engine/units.html), `+0x148`) [INFERRED: GDI runs that draw and the Vulkan lane skips
it]. **Open:** the CRT's `rand` `0x4E4870` for the runs that are not exact: its
stream is per thread and in neither recorded image, and a draw the Vulkan lane skips leaves the game
thread's stream elsewhere, so does any simulation step on the game thread draw from it
([sim](../engine/sim.html), *The two random streams*: 61 call sites)? The exact runs draw nothing
from the simulation RNG, so rendereq's answer does not reach it.
